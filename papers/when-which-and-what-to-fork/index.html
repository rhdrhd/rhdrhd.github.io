When, Which, and What to Fork: The Launch-Policy Design Space for LLM Agents

  • Zirui Wang* Huawei Edinburgh Research Centre
  • Mohamed M. Kassem Independent Researcher
  • Adam Barker Huawei Edinburgh Research Centre

* Work done while at Huawei.

PDF DOI Cite SoCC program

Abstract

Agents built on LLMs are becoming a major workload for inference-serving systems. Because a single attempt often fails, operators run many replicas of the same agent on each task. Each replica, however, is a stateful stream whose context grows over time. When many such streams share an engine, they can exhaust its fixed KV-cache memory and hit the memory wall: the entire batch slows down.

We argue that the missing control layer lies at the client: when to start each agent and from what state. We propose a launch policy that paces admission by running leaders first, publishes milestones from their trajectories as checkpoints, and then releases followers by forking from selected checkpoints. On SWE-bench Verified, across more than 200 graded runs, we compare our leader–follower policy with fixed-window launches under the same live-concurrency cap. On our main task, for batches of 64 agents, even when fixed-window uses its fastest tested concurrency cap of 26, our policy reduces batch completion time by 20% and yields 1.36× the solves per lane-hour, with similar mean resolve rates. For a larger batch of 128 agents with a concurrency cap of 32, switching to our policy reduces completion time by 37% and yields 1.7× the solves per lane-hour. These gains come from the complete policy, and the comparisons do not isolate the contributions of checkpoint inheritance and selection. Everything runs client-side on an unmodified engine.
